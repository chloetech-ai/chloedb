Benchmarks

First overall when one system has to do it all

Applications need full text, filters, SQL, vectors and hybrids together. We test all of them at once, against thirteen search engines and databases. ChloeDB is not the fastest at everything, and this page shows where.

How much faster or slower, engine by engine

ChloeDB's speed over each engine's, on the kinds both run. At 1M Qdrant and Milvus come closest at one client (ChloeDB is 1.3 times as fast on average, and slower than Qdrant on 6 of 11 kinds), and it is 1.6 to 11 times as fast as the rest; at 16 clients it serves 1.8 to 30 times each engine's queries a second. At 10M it is about twice as fast as Elasticsearch, OpenSearch and Vespa. Open an engine to see each kind, including the ones where ChloeDB is slower.

Where another system is clearly faster

The large losses, and what is being done about each.

Key lookups

10M · 0.99 ms, 6th of 9; Postgres 0.37

Postgres, Weaviate, ParadeDB and Qdrant read a row by id in under half ChloeDB's time, and Redis, on a bigger node, in a fifth of it. Most of ChloeDB's time is the hop from a coordinator to the shard over gRPC, not the lookup. #299 answers a pod's own shard inside its coordinator: 34% more lookups a second at 16 clients on the same nodes, already in these numbers. A lighter shard protocol would close more of the gap.

Typo-tolerant search

10M · 55.1 ms; Elasticsearch 32.4

A misspelt word is expanded against the dictionary. #242 and #266 took it from 824 to 45.8 ms at 10M and from 120 to 11.9 at 1M. Something since 7 November has slowed it to 55.1 and 16.5 ms, and it is not bisected yet. Elasticsearch is faster at 10M, and at 1M Meilisearch and Typesense take 7 ms. Next: a deletion index (SymSpell), in memory first.

BM25 × recency

10M · 8.02 ms, 3rd of 9; Vespa 5.42

Vespa and Qdrant are 1.4 to 1.5 times faster at one client at 10M, and at 1M Qdrant takes 2.21 ms against ChloeDB's 3.71. Planned, not started: skip the blocks whose recency bound cannot reach the top 10.

BM25 at 16 clients

10M · 650 q/s; OpenSearch 820

ChloeDB answers a single BM25 query the fastest at 10M (3.28 ms), but under load OpenSearch serves 26% more on the same machines, and Elasticsearch about as many (662). The shards' own scoring is what waits: moving the coordinators into the shard pods (#287) added 3% on the same nodes, and both layouts hit their CPU limits in bursts.

One client, through the coordinator

1M · BM25 2.03 ms on 0.3.5; 1.64 before #287

A client reaches a coordinator in a shard pod across the network, as with Elasticsearch: 4 to 29% slower at one client at 1M, 7% at 10M (median over the kinds), for up to 1.9 times the queries a second at 16 clients.

Vector recall at shared settings

10M · recall@10 0.798; Qdrant 0.941

ChloeDB scores 8-bit codes and re-scores the best 40, so read its vector speeds with this recall. At 1M, asking for 256 candidates instead of 64 lifts it from 0.809 to 0.931, and 512 to 0.957; at a little less recall than Qdrant's (0.931 against 0.944, 0.957 against 0.965) it serves 1.4 and 1.7 times Qdrant's queries a second, and with a filter 2.6 to 6.2 times as many at about equal recall. The sweep.

Short graph hops

Graph · 1 hop 0.35 ms over gRPC; Memgraph 0.18

Memgraph and FalkorDB, both in memory, answer one to three hops in about half ChloeDB's time. ChloeDB's own time on one hop is 0.10 ms against Memgraph's 0.02; gRPC instead of HTTP JSON took 0.1 ms off the rest. Not fixed yet.

Single-row writes in an app

268 to 800 commits/s; SQLite at FULL 933 to 1,340

On the 16 November node, whose disk syncs about five times slower than the earlier ones, ChloeDB commits single rows at 29 to 60% of SQLite's rate at FULL, and updates a price at less than half (552 a second with a text and a vector index, against 1,286). Its log only grows, and on that disk a sync that grows a file takes twice one that overwrites; its 6 November build is as fast there. Not fixed yet.

ChloeDB 0.3.5 against 7 November's image

Until 16 November these pages showed ChloeDB's 7 November image (861ab1d). At 1M, 0.3.5 ran on two m-4vcpu-32gb nodes as that image did, on another day. Its median at one client is 6 to 17% lower on 12 of the 14 kinds and the same on key lookups, and at 16 clients it serves between 4% fewer and 16% more queries a second. Typo search got slower, at 1M from 11.9 to 16.5 ms and 30% fewer queries a second at 16 clients, and at 10M from 45.8 to 55.1 ms, while Elasticsearch's and OpenSearch's typo queries did not. Something between 861ab1d and 0.3.5 slowed it; it is not bisected yet.

1M on two m-4vcpu-32gb nodes: Xeon Platinum 8168 on 7 November, 8168 and 8358 on 16 November. 10M in its report. Raw data: compare-msmarco-0.3.5-do and rerun-1107-do. The fixes of 5 and 7 November, each measured before and after on the same nodes: bigger-items-do and rerun-1107-do.

How we keep it fair

The same resources

Same CPUs, memory, data and vectors for every engine. At 1M every engine ran on 16 November, each lane's engines taking turns on the same machines. At 10M each engine had machines of its own; ChloeDB, Elasticsearch and OpenSearch ran again on 16 November, taking turns on the same two.

Answers checked

Recall@10 against exact search, and nDCG@10 where there are judgments.

Medians, not the best run

The median of three passes where there were three.

Every gap shown

A kind an engine cannot run counts as last place, and failed runs are reported.

Load not ranked

ChloeDB's shards were built offline, so load times are shown but not ranked.

Reproducible

Scripts, settings, data hashes and raw results are all in the repository.

Questions

Are we biased?

Probably. We build ChloeDB and wrote every engine's queries. Each engine runs at its defaults plus the tuning its documentation recommends, every setting is in the method, and we audited our own runs. If you know a better configuration, open an issue and we will run it again.

Why sum places instead of averaging speeds?

Latencies differ a thousandfold between kinds of query, so an average is decided by the slowest kind. Places weigh every test the same.

Doesn't counting a missing kind as last favour ChloeDB?

It favours breadth, which a mixed workload needs. At 1M ChloeDB also leads on the seven kinds every engine runs; at 10M Postgres leads on the four every engine answered (33 points against ChloeDB's 43). Each report shows its ranking under five rules.

Why only nine engines at 10M?

Five did not run in 52 GiB. Milvus and Redis then ran with twice the memory: shown, not ranked.

Can I run it myself?

Yes: How we test has the commands for a laptop or a cloud account.
