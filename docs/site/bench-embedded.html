No server · one file in an app · 2 and 3 November 2026

One file in your app, against the tools apps embed today

Many apps keep their rows in SQLite, their analytics in DuckDB, their vectors in LanceDB or FAISS and their search in Tantivy. Strata does all of it in one file. Here it runs as a library from Python against each of them, one system at a time on an otherwise idle 8-vCPU node: an app's reads and writes, search on five BEIR collections, writes and streams with indexes, and recommendations. Strata searches a query's segments on several threads by default, so the search charts also show an older Strata build held to one thread.

First on search, hybrid search, recommendations and reads beside a writer. Behind SQLite on single-row work and DuckDB on aggregates.

Machine
DigitalOcean g-8vcpu-32gb-intel (nyc3): Intel Xeon Platinum 8358 at 2.60 GHz, 8 vCPU, 31 GiB, Python 3.12.16
Systems
SQLite 3.53.1 with FTS5 and sqlite-vec 0.1.9, DuckDB 2.0.0 with fts and vss, LanceDB, FAISS beside SQLite, Tantivy 0.27.0
The app
A shop of 137,260 products and 4,624,176 reviews, with a text and a vector index on the products
Search
BEIR scifact, nfcorpus, fiqa, trec-covid and quora (3,633 to 522,931 documents), scored with nDCG@10 and recall@10
Method
Medians at one client after a warm-up, and queries a second at four client threads; one system at a time. Durability matched: Strata syncs every commit, as SQLite does at FULL

Search

Hybrid search faster than any other embedded system, on every collection

Strata's hybrid search (BM25 and vectors fused by RRF) is the fastest on every collection: 3.8 to 23 times the next system at its defaults, and 1.9 to 16.5 times on the four collections an older build ran on one thread, with nDCG@10 within 0.02 of the best. Vector search is 9 to 14 times faster than the next (4.2 to 13.8 on one thread), at a recall@10 of 0.986 to 0.999. On BM25 alone Tantivy is faster on the two smallest collections; Strata is faster on the two largest even on one thread, and on fiqa only with its default threads. Under each bar: the answer's nDCG@10, and recall@10 for vectors.

In this run Tantivy kept every word of a query while the others dropped Strata's 153 stopwords, which is most of its nDCG lead on quora; run again with the same stopwords, the two tie (below).

The app

Pages, joins and reads under writes faster than SQLite; single rows slower

SQLite fetches one row by id in 8 µs, against Strata's 64 µs through SQL and 21 µs through its key API, and it is faster at a user's few rows and their join. Against SQLite, Strata is 5 to 8 times faster at filtered, sorted list pages, deep pages included, about 5 times faster at the heavy join and at reads beside a writer, and 2 to 4 times faster at aggregates. DuckDB is the fastest at aggregates and the heavy join, and the slowest at single rows.

Writes and streams

Indexed writes, and reads that keep up with a stream

With a text and a vector index on the table, Strata commits one row at a time 2.3 to 2.5 times faster than SQLite and 7 to 12 times faster than DuckDB and LanceDB, and deletes fastest. SQLite and LanceDB take batches faster, and in this run updates to indexed text or a vector were Strata's worst case (LanceDB's are slower still). While 10,000 rows a second stream in, Strata still answers 6,424 reads a second, 6 to 12 times DuckDB's and SQLite's.

A later build on a newer CPU (the Tantivy run) made 1,797 title updates and 1,297 vector updates a second, with keyed updates (#223); SQLite was not run on that node.

Recommendations

Similar items in under a millisecond, under filters

Products similar to one the user viewed, in stock, in the price range, minus what they have seen. FAISS beside SQLite answers faster at the median, slower at the 99th percentile and with fewer queries a second; LanceDB takes 15 times longer. The home feed fuses vector similarity with a personalized PageRank over the graph of products reviewed together, in one SQL statement; LanceDB's feed, with the walk in app code, is 4.4 times faster.

Against Tantivy

Tantivy as the app's store, and BM25 side by side

Tantivy is a search library, not a database, but some apps keep their rows in it. Run on two newer nodes (Intel Xeon Gold 6548N, 8 vCPU) with a later Strata build: Strata commits single rows and updates 30 times faster and reads rows faster, while Tantivy aggregates and joins faster and loads big batches 2.8 times faster. With the same stopwords their BM25 answers tie on nDCG@10, and each is faster on some collections.

Where Strata is behind

Single rows, aggregates and one feed

  • One row by id, and a user's few rows. SQLite's 8 µs and 12 µs against Strata's 64 µs and 84 µs through SQL; a join over a user's reviews 22 µs against 657 µs. Strata's key API reads a row in 21 µs.
  • Aggregates and the heavy join. DuckDB counts reviews per month in 10.1 ms against Strata's 881 (SQLite: 3,569) and joins top products in 215 ms against 516.
  • One-row writes on plain tables. 4,022 rows a second against SQLite's 5,128 (FULL) and 11,091 (NORMAL); a price update 1,864 against 6,640 and 18,629.
  • The feed. 125 ms against LanceDB's 28.3: the PageRank walk over a 3.7M-edge table is 108 of its 113 ms, spent resolving keys and neighbours.
Raw data

Every number comes from these files

  • README.md: the machines, builds, caveats and every table
  • part-a/: one JSON report per system and part (a1_*, a2_*_<collection>, writes_*, a3_*)
  • part-a-tantivy/ and part-a-tantivy-2/: the Tantivy runs, each on its own node
  • strata-py/bench/laptop/: the scripts, and do_part_a.sh, which runs it all on one node
