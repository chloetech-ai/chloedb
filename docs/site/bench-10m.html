Cluster · 10M passages · DigitalOcean · 3 and 4 November 2026

Ten times the data, the same budget

The 1M run's machines and limits with ten times the data: the first 10M MS MARCO v2.1 passages with their Arctic vectors, every engine on DigitalOcean machines of its own with 6.8 CPUs and 52 GiB. Nine of the fourteen engines finished at those resources. Typesense and Milvus ran out of memory, and Meilisearch and Manticore were still loading after nine and eight hours. Milvus again, and Redis, which keeps everything in memory, ran on a 128 GB machine with the same CPUs: they are shown beside the others, not ranked.

Strata is first by a wider margin than at 1M. It is the fastest of the nine on 11 of the 45 tests, more than any other engine: both hybrids at the median and at 4 clients, facets at every measure, vectors at the median and the 99th percentile, filtered vectors and phrases at the 99th. It is in the top half on 34, one more than OpenSearch. Its two worst results are here too: a SQL aggregate took 4.3 s against OpenSearch's 4.86 ms, and a typo-tolerant query 824 ms against Elasticsearch's 32.7, the slowest of the ranked engines that ran them.

Data
MS MARCO v2.1 passages, the first 10,000,000, with Snowflake Arctic Embed M v1.5 vectors (768 dimensions) as published, checked against SHA-256 sums on every node
Machines
DigitalOcean sfo2, Kubernetes 1.37. Sharded engines: 2 × m3-4vcpu-32gb; one-node engines: 1 × m3-8vcpu-64gb; each a c-8 client of its own. Xeon Platinum 8168 and 8280, Gold 6248 and 5318Y, as handed out
Budget
6.8 CPUs and 52 GiB per engine as Kubernetes limits. Strata: 2 shards of 2.9 CPUs and 24 GiB, a coordinator of 1 CPU and 4 GiB
Queries
300 of 1,000 dev queries; every kind at 1, 4 and 16 clients for 6 s each, after warming up until two rounds' medians agree within 10%; one pass. Key lookups use each engine's own read by id
Noise
A second Strata on other nodes differed by 4.5% at the median and 19% at most: read gaps under 10% as ties
Quality
Recall@10 against an exact search over the same 10M vectors. No nDCG: the judgments cover 113M passages, not this slice

Overall

Summed places over 45 tests, the nine engines that finished

The same ranking under other rules

Strata stays first with throughput at 16 clients and with gaps under 15% counted as ties. Postgres comes first under the other two rules: on only the four kinds every engine answered (key lookups and the three vector kinds), and on the average place over the tests each engine ran. Its text kinds were stopped at a 20 s limit, which left it vectors, key lookups and SQL aggregates, and on the vector kinds it is the fastest at 4 clients.

The numbers behind the places

Every engine with results, Milvus and Redis on their 128 GB machine included but not counted in the places. Vector kinds show each engine's recall@10 beside its speed.

The 128 GB machine

Milvus and Redis, with twice the memory

Milvus ran out of memory building its index at 52 GiB, and Redis holds everything in memory, so both ran on an m-16vcpu-128gb with the same 6.8 CPUs and 110 GiB. Redis answers vectors and key lookups fastest of all, its vectors at a recall of 0.684, and took 0.8 to 1.5 s a query on BM25, facets, typos and hybrids. Milvus has the best filtered-vector recall, at about an eighth of Strata's vector throughput. Counting only the memory each process allocates itself, not the page cache, Redis held 72 GiB while searching, Milvus 64 GiB and Strata 2.2 GiB.

Writes and streams

Searchable in 8 ms, and a stream written once, at 10M

The 1M run's tests on the engines that shard; Solr's writes step failed waiting for a configuration change, so it has none. Strata and Vespa make a write searchable when it returns (8.2 and 10.0 ms), while Elasticsearch and OpenSearch refresh every second. Inserts are on a par with Elasticsearch and OpenSearch; updates are about twice as fast, but they do less work, since Strata sets the one column in place and the Lucene engines re-index the document. After the stream's consumer crashes, Strata resumes exactly where it was: it keeps the stream's offsets in the same transaction as its rows.

Load and size

Shown, not ranked

Strata's two shards were built offline, each on its node from local files, both at once; the others took their documents over HTTP. The times are not comparable, so the ranking leaves load out.

Where Strata is behind

Aggregates, typos, key lookups and recall

  • SQL aggregates. GROUP BY category … HAVING COUNT(*) > 1000 over a filter took 4.3 s, up from 178 ms at 1M. Elasticsearch and OpenSearch answer it from doc-value aggregations in under 8 ms. Not fixed yet.
  • Typo-tolerant search. 824 ms on this build. The typo rewrite merged after it (#242) cut a coordinator's median typed query from 112 to 15.8 ms on a Xeon VM; it has not been run at 10M.
  • Key lookups. Every engine used its own read by id. Strata's key API took 0.86 ms, about twice what Postgres, Weaviate, ParadeDB and Qdrant took.
  • Vector recall. At the shared settings (m 16, ef_construction 200, 64 candidates) Strata's recall@10 is 0.795, against Qdrant's 0.941 and Postgres's 0.826: read its vector speeds with that recall.
  • BM25 at 16 clients. 493 queries a second against OpenSearch's 777 and Elasticsearch's 608. The statistics cache that made it 51% faster in a container (#245) was not in this build.
What did not finish

Five engines that did not run at 52 GiB, and why

  • Typesense ran out of memory at 7.2M documents. A run on the 128 GB machine would have taken about eight hours and did not fit the budget, so it has no 10M numbers.
  • Milvus ran out of memory building its index after all 10M documents were sent, and ran again on the 128 GB machine.
  • Meilisearch had indexed about 4M documents when its nine hours ran out.
  • Manticore was still merging its index, two chunks left, after eight hours.
  • Redis keeps everything in memory (8.2 GB at 1M), so it ran on the 128 GB machine from the start.
  • Postgres finished, but its text search averaged 28 s a query on a 107 GB table that does not fit in its memory. A 20 s statement limit was set during the run so that its vector kinds could finish; its text kinds count as not answered.

Strata's first run hit its server's 5 s default deadline on the first cold vector queries and was run again with the 30 s the client gives every engine (#244). No number on this page comes from a failed attempt.

Raw data

Every number comes from these files

  • README.md: the setup, every job's machines, CPU check and outcome, the 128 GB lane, the noise check, and what went wrong on the way
  • tables.md: every table, both lanes, with each engine's overlap with Strata's BM25 top 10
  • rank.md: the ranking as rank.py computes it (Strata 133, OpenSearch 159, Elasticsearch 179)
  • The directory: run-*.json.gz per engine, loads, disks, writes, streams, CPU samples, and each job's log
  • fanout-jobs-10m.tsv and the method: the jobs, budgets, text analysis, vector settings and every engine's request for each kind
