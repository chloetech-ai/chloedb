Graph · arXiv citations · MacBook Air · 3 November 2026

Graphs beside your rows, against four graph databases

Strata keeps a graph as an EDGES field of a table's rows, or as an edge table, and walks it inside SQL. Here both layouts run against Neo4j, Memgraph, FalkorDB and ArangoDB on the ogbn-arxiv citation graph: 169,343 papers and 1,166,243 citations, 200 queries of each kind from one client, each engine alone in the same Docker VM with 3 CPUs and 6 GB. Every engine's neighbour and path answers matched an exact reference.

Strata's two layouts place first and second over 16 tests: 31 and 46 points, then Memgraph 60, Neo4j 67, FalkorDB 70 and ArangoDB 80. Without the six PageRank tests, which only Strata and Neo4j can run, it is close: Strata 21, Memgraph 24.

Machine
MacBook Air, Apple M2, 24 GB, macOS 26.0.1; Docker Desktop VM with 4 CPUs and 8.7 GiB
Budget
Each engine --cpus 3 --memory 6g, no swap; the client 1 CPU and 2 GB
Engines
Neo4j 2026.10.1 with Graph Data Science, Memgraph 3.14.0 (MAGE), FalkorDB 4.22.3, ArangoDB 3.12.4.3, Strata from main
Queries
200 seeds and 200 pairs per kind, one at a time, after 20 untimed; client time from send to the whole answer; two passes, the second reported
One laptopThe engines and the client share the VM's 4 CPUs on a fanless MacBook Air, so the numbers compare the engines with each other, not what any of them does on a server. Sub-millisecond medians moved by up to a third between earlier passes, less than either Strata's lead on shortest paths or its deficit on short hops.
Overall

Summed places over 16 tests

Client median and 95th percentile on six kinds of query, PageRank's recall at two settings, load time and memory, with Strata queried over gRPC. Each of Strata's layouts is ranked as a setup of its own, and a test a setup cannot run counts as last place (6th).

Latency

Short hops: the in-memory stores. Paths and PageRank: Strata

Memgraph and FalkorDB answer one to three hops in about half Strata's time at the median. Strata's shortest path is the fastest at the median and the 95th percentile, and so is its two-hop expansion at the 95th, where an average of 2,821 papers comes back. Its personalized PageRank takes 1.42 ms against Neo4j's 93.1, but it is an approximation: see how exact, below.

How exact

PageRank trades exactness for speed

These are different algorithms. Strata computes personalized PageRank by local push, which touches only the seed's neighbourhood, to a tolerance (eps); Neo4j's Graph Data Science runs power iteration over the whole graph. Against the exact top 20 Neo4j is closer at every setting, and no Strata setting reaches its 0.99: Strata's finest gets 0.955 in 52.6 ms, where Neo4j's coarsest gets 0.988 in 93.1 ms.

Load and footprint

Loaded in about a second, with the least memory and disk

The field layout keeps each paper's citations in its row, so it writes one row a paper. Stored one row a citation, as the other engines store their edges, Strata's edge table loads in 6.22 s against Memgraph's 6.96.
